## What is GR00T N1.5?
GR00T N1.5 is an improved version of [GR00T N1](/blog/gr00t-n1), with better generalization and language following ability. 

## The GR00T N1.5 Model
<GR00TArchitecture />

Structurally, nothing much is different from GR00T N1. It uses the NVIDIA Eagle 2.5 VLM to encode text and visual observations. The key differences from GR00T N1 are:

- The Eagle 2.5 VLM is used as the backbone.
- The VLM is frozen during both pre-training and fine-tuning.
- The adapter MLP connecting the vision encoder to the LLM is simplified, and adds layer normalization to both visual and text token embeddings input to the LLM.

### Different pre-training mixture
<DataMix />

The pre-training data mixture for GR00T N1.5 includes internal GR-1 data, OpenX-Embodiment, simulated GR-1 (DexMG), neural trajectories from DreamGen, and AgiBot-Beta. 

## Training GR00T N1.5
GR00T N1.5 uses Future Latent Representation Alignment (FLARE) in addition to the flow-matching loss used in GR00T N1. Instead of only learning what action to take from the current observation, FLARE encourages the model to predict what the future state will look like in its latent representation. In other words, the model is encouraged to think about “what will happen next?” before deciding what action to take.

During training, the model is given the current observation as well as a future observation. It learns to make its predicted future representation similar to the representation of the actual future observation. The future observation is only used during training, so no future information is required during inference.

This is particularly useful for learning from human videos, which generally do not contain robot action labels. Rather than requiring an exact robot action for every frame, FLARE allows these videos to teach the model about how the scene and objects change over time. This provides additional information that can help the robot learn manipulation behaviors.


## How does it perform?
| Benchmark | GR00T N1 (scratch) | GR00T N1.5 (scratch) |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Language table | 52.8% | **93.2%** |
| Sim GR-1 Language | 36.4% | **54.4%** |
<br/>
The N1 and N1.5 were trained from scratch on two simulation benchmarks that require language following. The N1.5 achieves significantly higher success rates on both benchmarks, indicating stronger language-conditioned control ability.
<br/>

| Simulation Benchmark | GR00T N1 | GR00T N1.5 |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| RoboCasa (30 demos per task) | 17.4% | **47.5%** |
| Sim GR-1 (zero-shot) | 39.6% | **43.9%** |
| Sim GR-1 (30 demos per task) | 43.2% | **47.4%** |
<br/>
As in GR00T N1, N1.5 is evaluated on data-limited post-training. For Sim GR-1, the model was evaluated both few-shot and zer-shot as the pre-training data includes them. Anyhow, the N1.5 records significantly better result in the very low data regime (zero-shot and 30 demos). 
<br/>

| Setting | GR00T N1 | GR00T N1.5 |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Language following rate | 46.6% | **93.3%** |
| Overall success rate | 43.3% | **83.0%** |

The results show that N1.5 significantly improves over the N1 in terms of language following on the real GR-1 robot.
